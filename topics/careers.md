## How the PhD changes over time

- **Years 1 to 3: exploration.** Learn as much as you can about different research, volunteer for service in your department and at conferences, meet as many people as possible, and say yes to almost every opportunity. This builds a network and helps you figure out what you are and aren't interested in.
- **Year 4: focus.** Spend your time on what relates to your research and goals.
- **Years 5 and 6: finish.** Focus on the thesis and your career, and say no to almost everything else.

## Research before coursework

- **Grades matter little.** Pass your classes and spend your energy on research ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/ways-to-fail-a-phd/); [Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm); [Azuma](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~azuma/hitch4.html)).
- **Start research instead of endlessly preparing.** Learning should serve your research ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/ways-to-fail-a-phd/)).
- **Read with the long term in mind.** Track every paper with notes on why it matters ([Fiesler](https://cfiesler.medium.com/advice-for-new-phd-students-your-research-career-is-a-long-game-4721abbd80ac)), and read recent well-regarded papers to build taste ([Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm)).
- **Know your reason for being there.** Without a strong reason for the PhD, a master's may be the better choice ([Azuma](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~azuma/hitch4.html)).

## Expect it to be hard

- **Disillusionment is normal.** Most students hit a low point when ideas fail and rejections pile up ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/the-shape-of-your-ph-d-426975399d88)). Research is harder than undergraduate work, technically and emotionally ([Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm)).
- **Volume closes the gap between taste and skill.** Your early work will disappoint you, and doing a lot of it is how it improves ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/the-shape-of-your-ph-d-426975399d88); [Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm)).
- **Drop perfectionism.** "Good enough" beats "perfect" ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/ways-to-fail-a-phd/)). Write rough first drafts and prototypes ([Marcus](https://marcua.net/writing/gradschool-guide/); [Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm)). Jean Yang learned that a paper can do much less than her first one and still be publishable ([Yang](https://jxyzabc.blogspot.com/2016/02/my-phd-abridged.html)).
- **Scope the dissertation.** Aim for a manageable contribution, keep it narrow, and save other ideas for later ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/ways-to-fail-a-phd/); [Fiesler](https://cfiesler.medium.com/advice-for-new-phd-students-your-research-career-is-a-long-game-4721abbd80ac)). Limit yourself to about two active projects ([Marcus](https://marcua.net/writing/gradschool-guide/)).
- **Persist before switching.** Give a project three to nine months before switching, and switch if it has stalled for a year ([Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm)).
- **Don't work alone.** Evan Jones's main regret was working by himself ([Jones](https://www.evanjones.ca/farewell-mit.html)). Collaboration gives accountability and momentum ([Marcus](https://marcua.net/writing/gradschool-guide/)).
- **How many hours?** Matt Might says a PhD doesn't fit a 9-to-5 schedule. Philip Guo says to treat it as a job with a fixed schedule and evenings off. Fiesler and Marcus warn against working every waking hour.

## Research taste

Early on, it's natural to judge your work by other people's opinions: your advisor, reviewers, the audience at a talk. Over time, develop your own sense of what good research is. Different communities value different kinds of work, so one community's judgment doesn't define the value of yours.

## Impact and visibility

- **Impact outlasts paper counts.** People remember the kind of work you do ([Fiesler](https://cfiesler.medium.com/advice-for-new-phd-students-your-research-career-is-a-long-game-4721abbd80ac)), and fewer high-impact papers beat many low-impact ones ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html)).
- **Make your work visible.** Keep a simple website with your CV and papers, and blog about your work ([Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/early-stage-PhD-advice.htm); [Fiesler](https://cfiesler.medium.com/advice-for-new-phd-students-your-research-career-is-a-long-game-4721abbd80ac); [Marcus](https://marcua.net/writing/gradschool-guide/)).
- **Build your network early.** Go to conferences and workshops from the start ([Marcus](https://marcua.net/writing/gradschool-guide/); [Azuma](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~azuma/hitch4.html)), and give talks at nearby universities when you travel ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html)).

## Academia or industry

People choose differently, for different reasons:

- Some go to industry because they see products used by millions as a more direct path to impact than academic publishing.
- Some choose academia because business priorities change fast, and they see people, especially students, as a more lasting legacy.
- Some choose academia for the freedom to pivot and to be judged by the interesting things they bring into the world.
- Many students won't become professors, and you "can't bet on an academic position" ([APA, Dear Advisor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2012/07-08/advisor); [Bailis](https://www.bailis.org/blog/i-loved-graduate-school/)). Choose the track where you create the most leverage ([Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)).
- Some care most about impact on policy or communities. That requires translating your work for those audiences and building relationships with them, beyond publishing.

## The academic job search

- **Plan backward and start early.** Begin a year before your start date, prepare materials over the summer and early fall, and apply from October through December ([Papernot and Redmiles](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vSeOnC_QdaJVc3OuuMfDHVlk3QotUxvghytRFaDsrdA0uovD5axQjp8kJCM4Evu1cCf9Hg_u_Stabu1/pub); [Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/); [Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html)). Finish the dissertation before interviewing if you can ([Ebling et al.](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rabi/N%20Things.PDF)).
- **Prepare strong materials.** Keep the cover letter short, write the research statement for a broad CS audience, take the teaching statement seriously, and keep your website current, since interviewers check it ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html); [Papernot and Redmiles](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vSeOnC_QdaJVc3OuuMfDHVlk3QotUxvghytRFaDsrdA0uovD5axQjp8kJCM4Evu1cCf9Hg_u_Stabu1/pub); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf); [Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/)). Track every school's deadlines in a spreadsheet.
- **The job talk decides the outcome.** Keep it to about 50 minutes, make it accessible to people outside your area, show what you personally did, and spend time on future work ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html); [Wing](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emigration/interview.pdf); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)). Practice with varied audiences and record yourself.
- **How much to rehearse?** Matt Might suggests about ten rehearsals before the first delivery. Jeannette Wing says at least twice, and warns that over-rehearsing makes a talk stale.
- **Treat interviews as two-way.** Research each person in advance, ask about their work, listen more than you talk, take notes each day, and never criticize one place to another ([Wing](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emigration/interview.pdf); [Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)). Do a lower-stakes interview first as practice ([Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/); [Ebling et al.](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rabi/N%20Things.PDF)).
- **Negotiate with a plan.** Build a startup budget with justifications, ask for a teaching release, and wait until all offers are in ([Papernot and Redmiles](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vSeOnC_QdaJVc3OuuMfDHVlk3QotUxvghytRFaDsrdA0uovD5axQjp8kJCM4Evu1cCf9Hg_u_Stabu1/pub); [Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/); [Ebling et al.](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rabi/N%20Things.PDF)). On salary, Ernst says there's little room to negotiate, while Wing says to aim high because raises build on it.
- **When to apply to industry?** Papernot and Redmiles say apply once academic interviews are nearly done; Might and Ernst recommend having an industry or postdoc backup in place.
- **Rejection is often about them.** Hiring depends on constraints you can't see ([Fiesler](https://cfiesler.medium.com/the-academic-job-search-its-not-you-it-s-them-41d3f4d47735); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)). Decline graciously and keep contacts, since the people you meet may become colleagues ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html)).
