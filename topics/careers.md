## How the PhD changes over time

- **Years 1 to 3: exploration.** Learn as much as you can about different research, volunteer for service in your department and at conferences, meet as many people as possible, and say yes to almost every opportunity. This builds a network and helps you figure out what you are and aren't interested in.
- **Year 4: focus.** Spend your time on what relates to your research and goals.
- **Years 5 and 6: finish.** Focus on the thesis and your career, and say no to almost everything else.

## Research taste

Early on, it's natural to judge your work by other people's opinions: your advisor, reviewers, the audience at a talk. Over time, develop your own sense of what good research is. Different communities value different kinds of work, so one community's judgment doesn't define the value of yours.

## Academia or industry

People choose differently, for different reasons:

- Some go to industry because they see products used by millions as a more direct path to impact than academic publishing.
- Some choose academia because business priorities change fast, and they see people, especially students, as a more lasting legacy.
- Some choose academia for the freedom to pivot and to be judged by the interesting things they bring into the world.
- Many students won't become professors, and you "can't bet on an academic position" ([APA, Dear Advisor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2012/07-08/advisor); [Bailis](https://www.bailis.org/blog/i-loved-graduate-school/)). Choose the track where your work has the most effect ([Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)).
- Some care most about impact on policy or communities. That requires translating your work for those audiences and building relationships with them, beyond publishing.

**Impact starts local.** Big-picture societal impact is hard to see or control, and thinking too far ahead gets stressful. Focus on what you can do for your local community and bubble, and the gratification is much more immediate. Let your interests and passions guide the rest.

## Teaching

From Jason Eisner's [guide to being a teaching assistant](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/advice/how-to-ta.html):

- **Prioritize discussion in sections.** Have students work problems in small groups and explain their answers, since listening alone may not produce learning.
- **Give fast, specific feedback.** Return feedback within a week, include praise, reuse comments for common errors, and share common mistakes with the class.
- **Grade fairly.** Agree on the rubric with the instructor, grade consistently, read answers charitably, never penalize twice for one error, and let a different TA handle regrades.
- **Be available and inclusive.** Hold office hours near due dates, route questions to the class forum, and protect student privacy.

## The academic job search

- **Plan backward and start early.** Begin a year before your start date, prepare materials over the summer and early fall, and apply from October through December ([Papernot and Redmiles](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vSeOnC_QdaJVc3OuuMfDHVlk3QotUxvghytRFaDsrdA0uovD5axQjp8kJCM4Evu1cCf9Hg_u_Stabu1/pub); [Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/); [Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html)). Finish the dissertation before interviewing if you can ([Ebling et al.](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rabi/N%20Things.PDF)).
- **Prepare strong materials.** Keep the cover letter short, write the research statement for a broad CS audience, take the teaching statement seriously, and keep your website current, since interviewers check it ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html); [Papernot and Redmiles](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vSeOnC_QdaJVc3OuuMfDHVlk3QotUxvghytRFaDsrdA0uovD5axQjp8kJCM4Evu1cCf9Hg_u_Stabu1/pub); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf); [Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/)). Track every school's deadlines in a spreadsheet.
- **Prepare the job talk carefully.** Keep it to about 50 minutes, make it accessible to people outside your area, show what you personally did, and spend time on future work ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html); [Wing](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emigration/interview.pdf); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)). Practice with varied audiences and record yourself.
- **How much to rehearse?** Matt Might suggests about ten rehearsals before the first delivery. Jeannette Wing says at least twice, and warns that over-rehearsing makes a talk stale.
- **Treat interviews as two-way.** Research each person in advance, ask about their work, listen more than you talk, take notes each day, and never criticize one place to another ([Wing](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emigration/interview.pdf); [Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)). Do a lower-stakes interview first as practice ([Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/); [Ebling et al.](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rabi/N%20Things.PDF)).
- **Negotiate with a plan.** Build a startup budget with justifications, ask for a teaching release, and wait until all offers are in ([Papernot and Redmiles](https://docs.google.com/document/u/1/d/e/2PACX-1vSeOnC_QdaJVc3OuuMfDHVlk3QotUxvghytRFaDsrdA0uovD5axQjp8kJCM4Evu1cCf9Hg_u_Stabu1/pub); [Might](http://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/); [Ebling et al.](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rabi/N%20Things.PDF)). On salary, Ernst says there's little room to negotiate, while Wing says to aim high because raises build on it.
- **When to apply to industry?** Papernot and Redmiles say apply once academic interviews are nearly done; Might and Ernst recommend having an industry or postdoc backup in place.
- **Rejection often reflects the department's needs.** Hiring depends on constraints you can't see ([Fiesler](https://cfiesler.medium.com/the-academic-job-search-its-not-you-it-s-them-41d3f4d47735); [Lazowska](http://lazowska.cs.washington.edu/jobs.pdf)). Decline graciously and keep contacts, since the people you meet may become colleagues ([Ernst](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/academic-job.html)).
