## How peer review works (CHI as an example)

- Papers chairs oversee subcommittee chairs, who each manage a group of associate chairs (ACs).
- Each AC handles a batch of papers with a second AC and external reviewers. Reviewers score papers, typically on a 1 to 5 scale, over a few weeks, and the AC writes a recommendation.
- Authors may get a short window to write a rebuttal, or a longer one for a revision.
- At the program committee meeting, low-scoring papers are usually not discussed. Accepted papers get time for final revisions and the camera-ready version.

Processes change from year to year and venue to venue, so check the current call for papers.

## Writing a review

- Summarize the paper's contribution in your own words first. If you can't, say so.
- Separate strengths from weaknesses, and say what the authors would need to address for you to support acceptance.
- Ask what specific claims are made, whether they are warranted by the evidence, and how the work relates to prior work.
- Be kind. You could be wrong, and the authors are more likely to act on feedback from someone who seems to be on their side.
- You can decline a review when you lack the expertise or are overloaded.

## Practice: a mock program committee

Review a paper on your own, then discuss it in a small group as if you were the committee. Compare your judgments with the actual reviews, read the authors' rebuttal and decide whether you would change your score, and then read the final version to see whether it delivered what the rebuttal promised.

## Rebuttals and revisions

- A rebuttal answers the reviewers' main concerns briefly and concretely, and says what you will change.
- In a revision, show exactly where and how you addressed each point. It is fine to push back on a reviewer when you have good reason.

## Rejection

Rejection is part of the job, and most good papers get rejected at least once. Different communities value different kinds of work, and developing your own sense of what good research is makes any one rejection matter less.
