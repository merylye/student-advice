## How peer review works (CHI as an example)

- Papers chairs oversee subcommittee chairs, who each manage a group of associate chairs (ACs).
- Each AC handles a batch of papers with a second AC and external reviewers. Reviewers score papers, typically on a 1 to 5 scale, over a few weeks, and the AC writes a recommendation.
- Authors may get a short window to write a rebuttal, or a longer one for a revision.
- At the program committee meeting, low-scoring papers are usually not discussed. Accepted papers get time for final revisions and the camera-ready version.

Processes change from year to year and venue to venue, so check the current call for papers.

## Reading a paper as a reviewer

- **Start with the abstract and introduction.** They should tell you what was done, why, and how. Compare those claims against what the paper delivers ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/a-quick-guide-to-reviewing-papers-quick-fe9a493ed228); [Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html)).
- **Read with questions.** List the questions you need answered to accept the paper, using criteria such as generalizability, replicability, validity, novelty, ethics, and impact ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/a-quick-guide-to-reviewing-papers-quick-fe9a493ed228)). Chan's core questions: Is the problem real? Are the contributions real? Does the theory hold? Are the experiments sufficient? ([Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html))
- **Judge the advance in knowledge**, and whether it is well grounded ([CVPR 2022 reviewer tutorial](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf)).
- **Stay within your expertise.** Skip criteria you can't judge, and tell the chair if you can't assess validity ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/a-quick-guide-to-reviewing-papers-quick-fe9a493ed228); [CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf)). You can decline a review when you lack the expertise or are overloaded ([Ko](https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/faq)).

## What a good review contains

- **Focus on the big picture.** Long typo lists and scattered nitpicks dilute your main message ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/a-quick-guide-to-reviewing-papers-quick-fe9a493ed228); [Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html)).
- **Give a reason for every judgment.** If you say the work isn't novel, cite the prior work ([CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf); [Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html)).
- **List strengths, weaknesses, and fixes.** Say whether the evidence supports the claims, check for fundamental flaws, and name what most needs improvement. Your unanswered questions can become revision requests ([CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf); [Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/a-quick-guide-to-reviewing-papers-quick-fe9a493ed228)).
- **Summary: long or short?** CVPR asks for a concise summary of the problem, solution, and contribution. Chan says to skip a long summary, since editors are experts. Das writes his summary as he reads to sharpen his questions.
- **Make a clear recommendation** and a strong case for it ([Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html)).

## Fair standards and tone

- **Don't invent requirements.** Don't reject a paper for failing to beat the state of the art, for lacking a theorem, or on novelty alone. "No paper is perfect" ([CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf)).
- **Don't penalize papers for discussing their limitations** ([CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf)).
- **Be compassionate and constructive.** Comment on the work, never the authors, and suggest how to improve the paper ([Das](https://sauvik-das.medium.com/a-quick-guide-to-reviewing-papers-quick-fe9a493ed228); [CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf)).
- **Be reliable.** Submit on time, read the rebuttal and say whether it changed your view, and send ethics concerns to the chairs ([CVPR 2022](https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf)).
- **Reviewing pays off.** It teaches you to write, builds relationships with editors, and gives you early access to new work ([Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html)).

## Practice: a mock program committee

Review a paper on your own, then discuss it in a small group as if you were the committee. Compare your judgments with the actual reviews, read the authors' rebuttal and decide whether you would change your score, and then read the final version to see whether it delivered what the rebuttal promised.

## Writing rebuttals

- **Structure it around the reviews.** Respond point by point in the reviewers' own terms, refer to reviewers by label, and put major points before minor ones. Bigham opens with thanks and a one- or two-sentence summary of the contribution ([Elmqvist](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/elm/2016/11/19/writing-rebuttals/); [Bigham](http://jeffreybigham.com/blog/2019/jeffs-rebuttal-guide.html)).
- **Answer every key concern**, and address each reviewer at least once ([Elmqvist](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/elm/2016/11/19/writing-rebuttals/); [Bigham](http://jeffreybigham.com/blog/2019/jeffs-rebuttal-guide.html)).
- **Promise small, specific changes.** Give exact details, say where each change goes, and explain what you'll cut to make room ([Elmqvist](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/elm/2016/11/19/writing-rebuttals/); [Bigham](http://jeffreybigham.com/blog/2019/jeffs-rebuttal-guide.html)).
- **Be polite, firm, and factual.** Never blame or insult reviewers, and avoid sarcasm ([Elmqvist](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/elm/2016/11/19/writing-rebuttals/); [Bigham](http://jeffreybigham.com/blog/2019/jeffs-rebuttal-guide.html)).
- **Admit weaknesses?** Elmqvist advises never admitting a major flaw and presenting concerns as needing minor clarification. Bigham advises accepting some limitations to build credibility.
- **Spend space where it counts.** Focus on undecided reviewers, and expect scores to move only a little ([Bigham](http://jeffreybigham.com/blog/2019/jeffs-rebuttal-guide.html)).
- **Fix the paper too.** If a reviewer misunderstood, fix the writing that caused it, and make every promised change if accepted ([Elmqvist](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/elm/2016/11/19/writing-rebuttals/)).

## Rejection

Most good papers get rejected at least once. Different communities value different kinds of work, and developing your own sense of what good research is makes any one rejection matter less.
