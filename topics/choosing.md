If you haven't gotten into grad school yet, here are my two cents:

> Don't apply to safeties. For each program on your list, ask yourself "if this is the only program that accepts me, would I be *happy* to go?" If the answer is no, take it off your list. You can always apply again next year. Your chances of graduating on time (or even finishing) decrease if you aren't in the right program, so you're not actually saving time by going to a safety.

## Deciding whether and when to apply

- **Start early.** Begin research one to two years before applying, and plan the application itself across most of a year, since some steps, such as letters, are out of your control ([Jin](https://medium.com/@zhijing-jin/applying-for-a-phd-in-nlp-9d070a02cda0); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Apply now or wait?** Might suggests applying soon after undergrad instead of taking a full-time job first. Smith calls it a personal choice and notes that working can build confidence. The NLP student panel warns against waiting only to add papers, since admissions are relative ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/)).

## Choosing where to apply

- **Choose by advisor and faculty fit before rankings.** Most guides put the advisor first and treat general rankings as a weak guide. Weigh strong researchers in your area, culture, funding, location, and computing resources ([Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit); [Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)). For checking publication output by area, see [CSRankings](https://csrankings.org) and [CSSRankings](https://cssrankings.org/#metric=weighted_if&window=10).
- **Look for more than one possible advisor at each school** so you can switch or co-advise later ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/); [Kuznetsov](https://blog.ml.cmu.edu/2020/03/02/questions-to-ask-a-prospective-ph-d-advisor-on-visit-day-with-thorough-and-forthright-explanations/)).
- **Build the list in stages.** Start from a long list, find one to three matching faculty per school, confirm they still publish and have current students, then narrow ([Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/)). For HCI and computational social science, also check related departments such as iSchools ([Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html)).
- **Check subfield strength.** Lower-ranked programs can be very strong in specific areas, though switching areas there may be harder ([Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Ask your mentors.** Faculty who know you can suggest a school list quickly and may contact colleagues on your behalf ([Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf); [Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/)).
- **Plan for money.** Budget for application fees, request fee waivers, and apply for every fellowship you qualify for, since fellowship deadlines come earlier ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/); [Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf)). See [Fellowships and Grants](../funding/).
- **How many programs, and safeties?** Other guides recommend a mix of reaches, matches, and safer schools, with counts from about 6 to 7 ([Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf)) to 8 ([CRA](https://conquer.cra.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/ThePhDinComputing_CRAEWebinar.pdf)) to 10 to 15 ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/)), and warn that applying only to top programs is risky. Krishnamurthi, like me, argues against treating backups as safety nets and says to choose every program by learning goals and faculty fit ([Krishnamurthi](https://parentheticallyspeaking.org/articles/us-cs-phd-faq/)).

## What committees look for

- **Research potential comes first.** Committees look for evidence you can do research, mainly through research experience, letters, and the statement ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Andersen](https://da-data.blogspot.com/2015/03/reflecting-on-cs-graduate-admissions.html)). Admissions work like hiring: faculty weigh whether to fund you ([Krishnamurthi](https://parentheticallyspeaking.org/articles/us-cs-phd-faq/)).
- **Publications help and are not required.** A clear narrative and strong letters can stand in for papers, and code releases and blog posts also count as evidence ([Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/)). Dettmers notes that in ML, first-author papers at top venues are becoming a stricter filter ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/)).
- **Someone must be willing to advise you.** Files are read by a few faculty in your area. A faculty advocate greatly raises your odds, and having no available advisor can sink a strong file ([Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf); [Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/)).
- **You need a reason to stand out.** Reviewers spend minutes per file, and a file with no clear strength is often rejected even with no flaws ([Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf); [Andersen](https://da-data.blogspot.com/2015/03/reflecting-on-cs-graduate-admissions.html)).
- **Grades and tests mostly act as thresholds.** The GRE is optional or ignored at many programs, and English-test minimums are firm ([Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/)). Check each program's current requirements.
- **How much do grades matter?** Might and Smith say they place little weight on GPA. Andersen's committee read a near-4.0 from a strong school as a meaningful signal, and Jin writes "GPAs do mean something." Lower-ranked programs may rely more on grades and scores ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit); [Andersen](https://da-data.blogspot.com/2015/03/reflecting-on-cs-graduate-admissions.html); [Jin](https://medium.com/@zhijing-jin/applying-for-a-phd-in-nlp-9d070a02cda0); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf)).
- **Don't reject yourself before the committee does** ([Krishnamurthi](https://parentheticallyspeaking.org/articles/us-cs-phd-faq/); [Hewitt](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~johnhew/undergrad-researchers.html)).

## Letters of recommendation

- **Letters are among the most important parts of the file.** Several guides rank them first ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/); [Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Specific beats famous.** A detailed letter from a less-known researcher who supervised you closely is worth more than a vague one from a well-known name ([Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf); [Andersen](https://da-data.blogspot.com/2015/03/reflecting-on-cs-graduate-admissions.html)). Connections between your writers and prospective advisors still help ([Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/)).
- **Letters from postdocs or grad students?** Harchol-Balter advises avoiding them unless a professor co-signs, and Fletcher says they weigh less. Nguyen says an enthusiastic letter from a postdoc who mentored you is much better than a generic letter from a famous person ([Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Fletcher](http://cwfletcher.net/Pages/SoP.php); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).

## The statement of purpose

- **Treat it as an argument for your research potential and fit.** Readers want evidence that you are prepared, focused on a subfield, and a match for the program ([Schneider](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80); [Weng](https://tinyurl.com/phd-sop-tips); [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/graduate-school-personal-statement/)).
- **Open with your research interest.** Lead with the area you want to pursue and why. The opening paragraph matters most ([Pavlo](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pavlo/blog/2015/10/how-to-write-a-bad-statement-for-a-computer-science-phd-admissions-application.html); [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/graduate-school-personal-statement/)).
- **Skip origin stories and favorite classes.** Childhood epiphanies, first computers, and generic quotes waste space ([Pavlo](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pavlo/blog/2015/10/how-to-write-a-bad-statement-for-a-computer-science-phd-admissions-application.html); [Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf)).
- **Describe each project with the same parts:** the problem and why it matters, your approach, your specific contribution, a challenge you faced, and the outcome or lesson ([Schneider](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Weng](https://tinyurl.com/phd-sop-tips)).
- **Back every claim with evidence.** Use a concrete example, name, or number, and avoid saying you are passionate or would be a great student ([Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf); [Schneider](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80)).
- **Write a narrative instead of restating your CV.** Explain why each experience mattered and how it led to the next, and include only what is relevant to the research you want to do ([Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Fletcher](http://cwfletcher.net/Pages/SoP.php); [Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_statement.html)).
- **Describe future directions with room to change.** Name subfields, topics, or methods. Schneider suggests placing yourself on axes such as quantitative vs. qualitative, which suits HCI and computational social science. The statement does not bind you; about a third of students change areas ([Schneider](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf)).
- **Name two or three faculty per school and explain the connection.** One name risks an advisor who isn't taking students, and long lists look unfocused. Check recent papers to confirm they still work on the topic. Naming faculty can route your file to them ([Schneider](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80); [Fletcher](http://cwfletcher.net/Pages/SoP.php); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Suggest how you would contribute**, such as extensions of a lab's projects or new problems of shared interest ([Chan](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_statement.html); [Weng](https://tinyurl.com/phd-sop-tips)).
- **Keep it short and plain.** Most guides set one to two pages. Use short sentences, active voice, and language a non-specialist can follow, and proofread carefully ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/graduate-school-personal-statement/)).
- **Revise over weeks with feedback.** Start two to three months ahead, get feedback from mentors and current PhD students, and send a near-final draft to letter writers a month before the deadline ([Fletcher](http://cwfletcher.net/Pages/SoP.php); [Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Handle weaknesses with care.** Explaining a weak semester is optional and can draw attention to it. A letter writer may explain it more credibly ([Schneider](https://nschneid.medium.com/inside-ph-d-admissions-what-readers-look-for-in-a-statement-of-purpose-3db4e6081f80); [Andersen](https://da-data.blogspot.com/2015/03/reflecting-on-cs-graduate-admissions.html)).
- **How much to tailor?** Dettmers reuses one statement and changes only the advisor section, and most of the NLP student panel kept 80 to 90% identical. Nguyen says a statement that can go to several programs with few changes is too generic ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Personal material?** Pavlo, MIT CommLab, and Nguyen advise against personal backstory. Sherry and the NLP student panel encourage a personal, honest voice, and Pavlo allows a personal story about something you built or investigated ([Pavlo](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pavlo/blog/2015/10/how-to-write-a-bad-statement-for-a-computer-science-phd-admissions-application.html); [Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/)).

Example statements are in the [library](../../library/?topic=choosing). You can also try the Before → Then exercise on [Exercises](../../exercises/).

## Contacting faculty before you apply

- **Read the professor's website first.** Many say how, or whether, to contact them ([Jin](https://medium.com/@zhijing-jin/applying-for-a-phd-in-nlp-9d070a02cda0); [Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/)).
- **If you write, make it short and specific.** Refer to their specific work, explain how your background connects, and never send mass or form emails. Don't ask a professor to predict your chances ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf)).
- **Meeting in person works better than cold email**, whether at a conference, a visit, or through an introduction from your own advisor ([Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/); [Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2018/11/26/phd-applications/)).
- **Does emailing help?** Might recommends emailing a month before applying, and Nguyen says contact can lead to faculty advocacy. Smith says emailing about your application will not improve your chances, Sap notes most professors won't reply, and most of the NLP student panel did not email. Faculty practices differ ([Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/); [Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf); [Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit); [Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/); [Jin](https://medium.com/@zhijing-jin/applying-for-a-phd-in-nlp-9d070a02cda0)).

## Evaluating a potential advisor

- **Talk to their current students.** Ask each of them how they work with the advisor and about the good and bad parts of the relationship.
- **Know your own working style.** Do you prefer working very collaboratively or more independently? Direct or less direct communication? Feedback through negative or positive examples? Hands-on management or independence? More independence is not always the right fit for your learning needs, even if you prefer it.
- **Check the fit with their research.** If your interests aren't core to an advisor's work, consider whether someone else fits better. Some faculty won't change scope much, and grants can constrain what projects are possible, so ask about funding.
- **Junior and senior advisors offer different things.** A newer professor may have more time, empathy, and openness to new areas, but fewer grants and connections. A junior and senior co-advising combination can balance this. Pre-tenure advisors also tend to be more ambitious, and tenure review can interrupt a PhD ([Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf); [Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html)).
- **Your first choice is not final.** Plenty of students switch advisors in their second or third year and still finish on time. The PhD is as much self-discovery as research.

### Do your homework

- **Read their recent work** and the lab website, including for professors outside your top choice ([Kuznetsov guide](https://www.andrewkuz.net/blog/PhD-Advisor-Guide.pdf); [Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf)). Check current projects so you can ask about specific ones ([Kuznetsov](https://blog.ml.cmu.edu/2020/03/02/questions-to-ask-a-prospective-ph-d-advisor-on-visit-day-with-thorough-and-forthright-explanations/)).
- **Check the track record.** Find how many students the advisor has graduated, how long it took, where they published, and where they work now. Compare across faculty in the same department ([Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist); [Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf); [Kuznetsov guide](https://www.andrewkuz.net/blog/PhD-Advisor-Guide.pdf)).
- **Learn the research style.** Ask "What are examples of research papers you like?" and think about whether you want to adopt the advisor's style ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/)).
- **How much should topics match?** Barres says only scientific and mentoring ability matter, and Dettmers adds that an advisor's values are more stable than their interests. Tasse advises arriving with more than a vague area because you will likely commit to one funded project ([Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf); [Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/); [Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html)).

### Questions to ask the advisor

- **Availability.** "Are you taking students this year?" What affects that decision? Is the position funded? Do they think your interests match? ([Kuznetsov](https://blog.ml.cmu.edu/2020/03/02/questions-to-ask-a-prospective-ph-d-advisor-on-visit-day-with-thorough-and-forthright-explanations/))
- **Advising style.** How often do you meet, and what do meetings cover? Who supervises day-to-day work? What progress do they expect each semester? How do they decide to pivot a project? ([Kuznetsov](https://blog.ml.cmu.edu/2020/03/02/questions-to-ask-a-prospective-ph-d-advisor-on-visit-day-with-thorough-and-forthright-explanations/); [Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist))
- **Support.** What do they do when students struggle, and what have they done in the past? Kuznetsov's checklist adds "When have you given a letter of concern? Why?" ([Kuznetsov guide](https://www.andrewkuz.net/blog/PhD-Advisor-Guide.pdf))
- **Funding and obligations.** What grants do they have, what would you work on, what duties come with the funding, what happens if it runs out, and how do they view fellowships? ([Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html); [Kuznetsov](https://blog.ml.cmu.edu/2020/03/02/questions-to-ask-a-prospective-ph-d-advisor-on-visit-day-with-thorough-and-forthright-explanations/))
- **Plans and careers.** Any sabbaticals, moves, or retirement in the next several years? Do they support the career you want, including industry, internships, and conference travel? ([Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html); [Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist))
- **How to ask.** Asking is legitimate. Be confident and respectful, keep the number of questions reasonable, and notice whether the advisor engages or seems put off ([Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist)).

### Talking to students

- **Talk to former students too, without the advisor present.** Former members can answer harder questions ([Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist); [Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf)).
- **Ask about behaviors instead of labels.** "Is my advisor good?" or "Are they hands-on?" rarely separates advisors. Ask about feedback on writing, meeting cancellations, email response time, check-in frequency, and how the advisor acts on bad-news days ([Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html); [Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/)).
- **Ask about pressure and duties:** how much the advisor shields students from funding pressure, and how much time goes to non-research work ([Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html)).
- **Ask whether students are happy**, whether the lab has team spirit or competition, and whether lab meetings are real discussions ([Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf); [Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist)).

### Lab culture and warning signs

- **Peers matter almost as much as the advisor.** Notice whether you click with the group and whether postdocs or senior students can help ([Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/)).
- **Be cautious about very large or postdoc-heavy labs**, since mentoring takes time ([Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf)).
- **Warning signs:** advisors who don't credit students by name, put students in competition on the same project, dictate every step, sit on manuscripts, or expect extreme hours ([Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf)).
- **Ask how women and minority students have fared** in the lab, and whether the advisor is aware of their own biases ([Carpenter](http://www.sciencemag.org/careers/2007/07/choose-adviser-be-armchair-anthropologist); [Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf)).
- **Ambition or balance?** Tasse says neither style is better. Barres favors advisors who expect a balanced life over 60-plus-hour weeks. Dettmers asks you to decide whether you need an advisor who prioritizes well-being or progress ([Tasse](https://talesnideas.blogspot.com/2014/08/phd-grind-part-1-of-n-advisor-picking.html); [Barres](https://hst.mit.edu/sites/default/files/media/files/Barres%20BA.Neuron.80.275.2013.pdf); [Dettmers](https://timdettmers.com/2022/03/13/how-to-choose-your-grad-school/)).

## Interviews, visits, and deciding

- **Treat interviews as conversations.** Expect questions about a past project and your interests. Know everything in your statement, explain your work from the basics, and admit what you don't know ([Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/); [Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit)).
- **At visit days, talk to students most**, and use the same question list at every school ([Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/); [Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf)).
- **Check structural details:** funding guarantees, qualifying exams, completion rates, and whether advisor assignment is guaranteed ([Harchol-Balter](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~harchol/gradschooltalk.pdf); [Liu et al.](https://blog.nelsonliu.me/2019/10/24/student-perspectives-on-applying-to-nlp-phd-programs/)).
- **Trust your gut** once you have the information ([Sap](https://maartensap.com/notes/01-apply-grad-school.html); [Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf)).
- **Advisor fit or a big program?** Nguyen calls "go where the advisor fits" hard to act on and recommends a large, well-known program if you have no strong preference, partly because switching advisors is easier there. Smith and Jin put the individual advisor ahead of the school ([Nguyen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nguyenthanhvuh/phd-cs-us/main/demystify.pdf); [Smith](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lT-bsIP0GKfh8l5sQnM2hCzzR9prt-QLx16rimUOdIM/edit); [Jin](https://medium.com/@zhijing-jin/applying-for-a-phd-in-nlp-9d070a02cda0)).
- **Close the loop.** Don't accept unfunded offers. Send thank-you notes and tell schools your decision by April 15. If rejected, ask for feedback and consider reapplying ([Krishnamurthi](https://parentheticallyspeaking.org/articles/us-cs-phd-faq/); [Sherry](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~justine/advice.pdf); [Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/how-to-apply-and-get-in-to-graduate-school-in-science-mathematics-engineering-or-computer-science/)).

## Co-advising

Common reasons to be co-advised:

- The co-advisors share the grant that funds you.
- Your work spans disciplines that no single advisor covers.
- You are moving from one discipline into another.

**Good signs** when two faculty haven't co-advised before: they have co-advised students together, write grants together, understand and respect each other's methods, are excited by the idea, can see how it benefits you, and like each other as people.

**Bad signs:** confusion or hesitancy at the suggestion, no clear reason you need both of them, a lack of respect for each other's methods, or agreeing too quickly without due caution.

**Agree on the terms up front.** Settle each advisor's role, who funds you long term, authorship expectations, and how disagreements between advisors will be resolved.

**Timing.** Add co-advisors before your thesis proposal. Adding one is much easier than dropping one, so only add someone you intend to keep, and don't have more than two advisors.

## Co-advisor or collaborator?

Collaborating with another professor gives you many of the same benefits without the commitment: mentorship your advisors can't offer, another letter writer and committee member, and evidence that you work well with different people. If it doesn't work out, you don't have to work with them again.
