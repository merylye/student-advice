## Why and when to apply

- **Fellowships give you freedom.** Funding that follows you frees you from TA duties and grant-specific work, and widens your choice of thesis topic ([Strouse](http://djstrouse.com/guide-to-applying-to-us-science-phd-programs-and-fellowships/)).
- **Start at least two months before the deadline**, and update your CV before you write. The NSF GRFP deadline usually falls in October or November, earlier than most grad school deadlines ([Strouse](http://djstrouse.com/guide-to-applying-to-us-science-phd-programs-and-fellowships/); [Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp)). Check current dates.
- **Know the options.** Major US fellowships include NSF GRFP, Ford, NDSEG, DOE CSGF, and Hertz ([Strouse](http://djstrouse.com/guide-to-applying-to-us-science-phd-programs-and-fellowships/)). Federal awards generally can't be combined.
- **Check eligibility in the current program solicitation.** GRFP rules about who can apply and how many times have changed over the years, and older guides conflict.

## Writing a fellowship proposal

- **Learn from successful proposals.** Read several and synthesize a template of the elements they share and what they do well.
- **Brainstorm honest signals for each section.** For each part of your outline, list as much concrete evidence as you can of your ability and commitment. Overgenerate and prune later.
- **Open with your research vision.** Use an and-but-therefore structure: the state of the world, the problem, and what your work will do about it.
- **Tell your origin story.** Explain what drives you, and thread it together with your opening paragraphs so the proposal has an air of inevitability.
- **Address broader impacts directly.** Know what the personal statement is for and how the fellowship evaluates broader impacts and diversity.
- **Get feedback, and give it.** Review classmates' drafts and build up a lexicon of hedges you can cut from your own.
- **Proofread carefully.**

## Using the review criteria (NSF GRFP)

- **Read the program solicitation first.** Formatting errors can get an application rejected without review. Keep the instructions beside your draft as you edit ([Safavi](https://tsafavi.github.io/nsf-grfp.html); [Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp)).
- **Address Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts explicitly**, with headers for both in each essay ([Safavi](https://tsafavi.github.io/nsf-grfp.html); [Lang](https://www.alexhunterlang.com/nsf-fellowship); [Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp)).
- **Make Broader Impacts concrete and personal.** Describe specific outreach, teaching, mentoring, or public communication and your role in it. Every guide treats this as central ([Strouse](http://djstrouse.com/guide-to-applying-to-us-science-phd-programs-and-fellowships/); [URI Graduate Writing Center](https://web.uri.edu/graduate-writing-center/tips-for-the-nsf-grfp-application/); [Safavi](https://tsafavi.github.io/nsf-grfp.html); [Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp)).

## The research proposal

- **Propose something concrete you know well**, feasible in about three years. Reviewers assess your knowledge and clarity, and you aren't bound to carry out the exact plan ([Strouse](http://djstrouse.com/guide-to-applying-to-us-science-phd-programs-and-fellowships/); [Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp); [Lang](https://www.alexhunterlang.com/nsf-fellowship)).
- **Show novelty, rigor, and self-critique.** Confirm the question is open, state a hypothesis and two or three independent aims, explain your methods and what each outcome would mean, and acknowledge limitations ([Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp); [Safavi](https://tsafavi.github.io/nsf-grfp.html)).
- **Write for a general science reader** who knows your field but not your subfield ([Lang](https://www.alexhunterlang.com/nsf-fellowship)).
- **Figures?** URI advises one relevant figure because space is limited; Safavi says "Don't be afraid to use figures"; Liu says use them only when they clearly help.

## The personal statement

- **Tell a story with evidence.** Use concrete examples instead of adjectives about yourself, organize around a theme, and avoid clichéd openings ([Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp); [URI](https://web.uri.edu/graduate-writing-center/tips-for-the-nsf-grfp-application/)).
- **Don't undersell yourself.** Bold key results, awards, and contributions for busy reviewers, and use clear topic sentences ([Safavi](https://tsafavi.github.io/nsf-grfp.html); [Liu](http://www.christineliuart.com/writing/2018/8/31/advice-for-applying-to-the-nsf-grfp)).
- **How much research to include?** URI says the personal statement should focus on you, with research mentioned to show qualifications. Strouse and Safavi put more weight on research.

## Funding and finances

- Funding shapes which projects are possible. Ask potential advisors how students in their group are funded.

### Stipends

- **Compare stipends against cost of living.** [PhD Stipends](https://www.phdstipends.com) collects thousands of self-reported stipends by school and department. Pair it with the [MIT Living Wage Calculator](https://livingwage.mit.edu) for the city. A 2022 analysis that combined the two found MIT's stipends fell around the 80th percentile once adjusted for cost of living, so a higher number on paper can mean less in practice ([Stipends analysis](https://web.mit.edu/julianst/www/phdstipends/stipends.html)).
- **Self-reported data is noisy.** Treat these numbers as a starting point, and confirm the current stipend, summer funding, and fees with the program and its students during visits.

### Money

David Andersen's [advice on finances for CS PhD students](https://da-data.blogspot.com/2016/09/finances-for-cs-phd-students.html) (from 2016, so check current limits and figures):

- **Spend less than your stipend**, and save most of any internship pay.
- **Consider a Roth IRA.** Grad school is usually your lowest tax bracket, so after-tax retirement savings are a good deal. Check whether your school offers a Roth 403(b).
- **Invest simply.** Use low-cost index or target-date funds, and avoid day-trading, market timing, and stock picking.
- **Plan for transition costs.** Keep savings for moving and deposits after graduation, think hard before buying a home, and pay down high-interest debt.

### Conference travel

- **Volunteer.** CHI's student volunteers get conference registration waived plus breakfast and lunch on site, in exchange for about 20 hours of work. Undergraduate, master's, and PhD students are eligible. Most spots are filled by lottery through the [CHISV system](https://new.chisv.org), which opens months before the conference. Housing and travel are not covered ([CHI 2026 call for SVs](https://chi2026.acm.org/2025/10/16/call-for-svs/)). Many other ACM conferences run similar programs.
- **Apply for SIGCHI's Gary Marsden Travel Awards.** Students, early-career researchers, and people facing financial hardship or lacking institutional support can apply. The award covers registration and reimburses travel costs after the conference, and applications are judged on financial need and benefit to you. There are seven deadlines a year (the 9th of January, February, March, May, July, September, and November), and you must apply more than a month before the conference ([SIGCHI](https://sigchi.org/resources/gary-marsden-travel-awards)).
- **Look for conference-specific student travel grants.** Many conferences, such as ICWSM and CSCW, announce their own student travel grants on their websites, often with deadlines well before the event.
- **Attend funded workshops.** The CRA-WP Grad Cohort Workshop is free for master's and PhD students in computing at North American institutions, and reimburses airfare, ground transport, meals, and a shared hotel room. Priority goes to first-time attendees. It covers finding a research topic, publishing, working with your advisor, and careers. Applications open in the fall ([CRA-WP](https://cra.org/cra-wp/grad-cohort-workshops/)).
- **Check your university and department.** Many schools fund student conference travel separately from research grants. At CMU, for example, the Graduate Student Assembly runs conference funding, and Scholarly Project Funding gives $750 for research costs such as datasets, software, participant fees, and research travel, excluding conference travel ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/graduate/funding/research-grants.html)).
- **Plan for reimbursement delays.** Most travel funding pays you back weeks after the trip, so you may need to cover airfare and hotels upfront. Some programs, including SIGCHI's awards and CRA-WP, can pay costs in advance if you ask.
