I use AI regularly, mostly for coding and organization, but I *try* to maintain human oversight, read over everything, and cross-check new factual claims.

## Guidelines for research tasks

From Kathleen Carley's practicum talk on literature reviews:

- **Don't use LLMs to find references.** They make up literature, favor work that is popular in the press, and misattribute credit.
- **Use them where you can check the output.** Summarizing papers, labeling content, translating, drafting to get past writer's block, and coding are all reasonable uses if you verify the result.
- **They are limited by you.** LLMs can suggest trends or possible gaps, but only within what you already know to ask.
- **Watch for the common failures:** hallucination, overemphasis on popular papers, an inability to tell scientific from non-scientific material, and superficial synthesis.
- **Know your venue's rules.** Journals, conferences, and funders have policies on AI-generated text and on using AI in peer review.

## Keep your understanding ahead of the tool

From Eytan Adar's essay [AI-Native PhD Students](https://eytanadar.medium.com/ai-native-phd-students-f9f6eebc1f91):

- **Use AI in proportion to what you understand.** Rely on it least for the work you find hardest, and don't use it to avoid learning.
- **Own your key decisions.** Be able to explain and defend each important choice, such as your analysis method, and say what would falsify your result.
- **Build deep domain knowledge.** Expertise lets you lead how your field changes in response to AI.
- **Treat AI agreement with suspicion.** AI tools tend to agree with you, so their claims that your work is rigorous are not evidence.
- **Ask what you add.** Heavy AI use can make different students' work look alike and adds to a flood of low-quality submissions.
- **Invite oversight.** Ask mentors and peers to hold you accountable for how you use AI.
- **Expect to be tested on understanding.** Adar expects more oral defenses and in-person exams, and job talks where you'll have to defend your work without help.
