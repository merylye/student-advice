## Getting involved

- **Be proactive.** Don't take it personally if faculty/PhD students aren't approaching you. Initiate contact, come to office hours, and ask for specific things. It is much easier for a mentor to respond to a specific request than to a vague ask for mentorship.
- **Find multiple mentors.** Rather than searching for one perfect mentor, build a team where each person provides something you need. Mentors can be faculty, staff, or other students.
- **Read your mentor's papers first.** Before you meet, read two or three of their recent papers and think about why the work matters. It gives you something specific to talk about and shows you've done the work.
- **Ask about the unwritten rules.** Much of how research works, from authorship norms to how often to check in, is never written down. Ask a senior student in the lab what they wish they had known when they joined.

## What research is like

- **Research is different from coursework.** There's no answer key, and most of your early attempts won't work. That's normal. The skill you are building is figuring out what to try next.
- **Expect to spend time confused.** Not knowing what you don't know is part of starting out. Asking questions early saves weeks of going in the wrong direction.
- **Treat meetings as a chance to get help.** Bring the problems you are stuck on, not only the things that went well.

## Evaluating good research

A quick way to understand any paper, including your own project, is to answer five questions:

1. What is the problem or gap, and why is it interesting and important?
2. What are current approaches, and why are they not adequate?
3. What is new about this approach? Why is it impactful? Why will it be better? How does it work?
4. What did they find, why is it important, and what are the shortcomings and limitations?
5. How does it generalize? What are the new directions, implications, and next steps?

## Thinking about a PhD?

A PhD is a job where you are trained to become an independent researcher. Talk to current PhD students about what their days look like before you decide. If you're applying, see [Choosing a program and advisor](../choosing/).
