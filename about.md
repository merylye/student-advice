I'm Meryl Ye, a PhD student in Societal Computing at Carnegie Mellon University. I started this collection as a Google Doc for early PhD students and undergrads interested in research, and turned it into a site so it would be easier to navigate.

The advice here is written for all PhD students, with a tilt toward computer science, HCI, and computational social science, because that's what I know best.

## Credits

This site draws on:

- [awesome-phd-advice](https://github.com/pliang279/awesome-phd-advice), Paul Liang's compilation of PhD advice, which supplied many of the links in the library.
- The Process & Theory seminar for first-year PhD students in CMU's Human-Computer Interaction Institute, taught by Niki Kittur, and the advice its students and panelists shared.
- Talks from the CMU Societal Computing practicum, including Andrew Begel on advisor relationships, Hanan Hibshi on work-life balance, Kathleen Carley on literature reviews, and Sarah Scheffler and Nicolas Christin.
- The many people whose blog posts, guides, and memoirs are linked throughout.
